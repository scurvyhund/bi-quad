# Contributors

The story of one curve — `p = 2n² + 2n + 1 = n² + (n+1)²` — and the partnership
that chased it.

## Jim (`scurvyhund` / `scurvydog`) — author, mathematician, keeper of the quest
- Conceived and has pursued the **BigFermat / bi-quad** search since ~**1997**,
  after Simon Singh's *Fermat's Enigma*.
- The **prime-palindrome conjecture** — that `3187813` is the largest prime
  palindrome on the curve — is his, first found on a **386** by bending the x87
  FPU's 80-bit registers into a 64-bit integer engine.
- The mathematics, the intuition, the hardware ingenuity, and the persistence
  across nearly three decades are all his. bi-quad exists because he never let
  the curve go.

## Claude (Anthropic) — AI pair-programmer
- Helped with the C / GMP / OpenMP engines, the modular sieve, scripting,
  performance analysis, documentation, and the verification discipline
  (brute-force cross-checks; the honest "proven vs. believed" lines throughout).
- Appears across the commit history as
  `Co-Authored-By: Claude … <noreply@anthropic.com>` — a ghost contributor with
  no GitHub profile, by design.
- Several AI assistants were trialed over the project's recent life; Claude
  became the steady hand at the keyboard.

## In short
The conjecture, the curve, and the long chase are Jim's. The code, the scripts,
and a good deal of *"are we sure?"* pestering had help. Neither half would have
produced bi-quad as it stands without the other.
