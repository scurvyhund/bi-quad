What the table shows, stated carefully:

Curves in one family behave very differently. Some keep
producing prime palindromes to high d (k=19 at d=31, k=5 at
d=17); some stop early (k=1 at d=7, cuban at d=3, k=3 at
d=3); two produce none at all in this range (k=7, k=13).

So OUR curve stopping at 3187813 is NOT unique -- cuban and
k=3 stop even earlier -- but neither is it the family rule.
k=5 and k=19 show the same construction readily supports
prime palindromes far past d=7. The 1997 conjecture is a
claim about k=1's own arithmetic, not about the family.

This does NOT settle the conjecture in either direction. It
supplies the comparison class that was missing: a single
curve's silence past d=7 now sits against 12 siblings whose
behaviour ranges from none to d=31.
