### 1 — the filtered alarm, in detail

`hunt.c` checks the even-d mod-11 invariant on every survivor and was
compiled into the resweep binary (verified present in `2d1948a`). It
reports via `fprintf(stderr, "  *** BUG d=%d: ...")`. The 2026-09-05
wrapper piped stdout+stderr through `grep -E "range=|wall=|EMIRP"`,
which that line does not match — tested directly.

Even-d survivors existed for it to check: d=16 (1), d=24 (1), d=26 (4).
Had any violated the invariant, the alarm would have been discarded.

This was not an oversight so much as a **coupling nobody owned**.
`docs/mod11_converse_constraint.md:233` and commit `307599d` both
deliberately chose "loud stderr diagnostic, never abort()", reasoning
that a 100-hour run should not die on a check. Sound reasoning — but
`abort()` would have been caught, since both wrappers gate on the
result line being present, while a filtered stderr line is silent.
The gentler choice was the undetectable one.

Recovered for free: the theorem's palindrome half IS confirmed from the
logs, because it rides the `range=` line — every even d reports
`palindromes=0`, d=14 through d=26. d=14's 6 survivors were re-verified
2026-09-11 under the fixed script (0 BUG lines). What is open is the
residue condition on 6 survivors at d=16, 24, 26.

Fix (phase 5): carry a `mod11_violations=N` field in `hunt`'s SUMMARY
line, so the alarm rides the one channel every wrapper already keeps
and gates on. Keeps the non-fatal behaviour; removes the dependency on
stderr surviving.
