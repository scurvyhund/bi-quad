Three Lattices,
One Construction

Our curve, the cuban curve, and the Z[√−2] curve look like three different formulas. Geometrically they are one idea repeated in three different lattices: the lattice points that sit on a ray, at squared distance p from the origin.

First, a correction

Not a sphere, not a cone — a paraboloid

There is a real 3-D surface here, and it is worth being exact about which one, because the three candidates behave very differently.

surfaceequationwhat it is
spherex² + y² + z² = r² closed, bounded. Nothing here is bounded — p runs to infinity.
conez² = x² + y² z appears squared. Straight sides, a sharp apex.
paraboloidz = x² + y² ours. z appears to the first power, so the sides curve upward. A bowl, not a funnel.

The whole curve is one slice of that bowl. Put z = p and the surface z = x² + y² is every pair whose squares sum to p. Now cut it with the vertical plane y = x + 1 — the "consecutive" condition. The cut is a parabola, and its equation is exactly our curve.

"Conic section" is literal here. A conic is what you get when a plane cuts a quadric surface. Our curve is a plane cutting a paraboloid. That is not an analogy — it is the definition, and it is why the curve has genus 0 and no group law.

The construction

Every form is a circle in its own lattice

Look down on that bowl from above and the level sets are circles. A curve point is a lattice point sitting exactly on one, and on the ray y = x + 1.

The three curves differ only in which lattice you count on. Each quadratic form is the squared distance in its own ring's natural lattice — verified below to 13 places for every point within radius 6.

Z[i] · square

x² + y²

p = 2n² + 2n + 1

Gaussian integers. The lattice is the ordinary square grid, so the level sets are true circles. Ours. 1, 5, 13, 25, 41, 61

Z[ω] · triangular

x² + xy + y²

p = 3n² + 3n + 1

Eisenstein integers. Hexagonal packing. This curve is also (n+1)³ − n³ — the cuban primes. 1, 7, 19, 37, 61, 91

Z[√−2] · rectangular

x² + 2y²

p = 3n² + 4n + 2

A grid stretched by √2 vertically. Level sets are circles in that stretched metric. 2, 9, 22, 41, 66, 97

Why they look like ellipses if you draw them on graph paper. Plot x² + xy + y² = p against ordinary square axes and you get an ellipse with axis ratio √3. That ellipse is an artefact of forcing a triangular lattice onto square paper. In its own lattice it is a circle.

The two dials

What each constraint does to the geometry

A curve here is a quadratic form plus a linear condition. Both are geometric, and each does one visible thing. The other two conditions in this project do nothing visible at all — which is the whole point.

constrainteffect on the picturegeometric?
quadratic form picks which lattice you count on — square, triangular, rectangular yes
linear condition picks which ray through the lattice — y = x+1, y = x+k yes
primality thins the surviving points, following no visible pattern. Nothing in the picture distinguishes a prime hit from a composite one no
digit reversal none whatsoever. rev(12641) = 14621 is invisible to distance, angle, or lattice no

That table is the answer to "does the geometry constrain the math." The geometry hands you the curve for free — it is a plane cutting a bowl, and every question about the curve as a curve is answered. Then two conditions arrive from outside the geometry entirely and supply all of the difficulty.

Which retro-explains three dead ends. MITM, two-ended digit-DP, and the 50-modulus congruence sweep all attacked structure. But the structure is the part that was never hard. Attacks aimed at the curve will keep failing, because the curve is not where the problem lives.

The one place they touch

Where base 10 reaches the algebra

Digit reversal has exactly one algebraic handle, and it exists because 10 ≡ −1 (mod 11). Powers of ten then alternate +1, −1, +1, −1, and reversing a d-digit number becomes a single sign flip:

rev(p) ≡ (−1)d−1 · p  (mod 11)

At even d that is p + q ≡ 0, which is strong enough to eliminate 7 of the 11 residue classes and to rule out even-digit palindromes on most of these curves. At odd d it collapses to a² ≡ b² and says almost nothing.

Both objects we actually care about — the emirp 12641 ↔ 14621 at d = 5, the palindrome 3187813 at d = 7 — sit at odd d, on the toothless side.
